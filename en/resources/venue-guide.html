01 The right time

When to film

It isn’t your venue. It’s the time you show it.

Most people film their venue when they have a spare moment, and the spare moment tends to be the hour when nothing is going on. There are three moments in the day that do the work for you. Each one is good for something different.

The blue hour moves through the year. In Madrid the sun sets around six in December and around ten in June. Check the sunset time in your phone’s weather app and add twenty minutes.

  1. 01

    In the morning

    Before opening

    Products and details

    With the venue closed and daylight coming through the window. It is the cleanest side light you will get: the plate, the glass or the display next to the window, with no rush and nobody in the way.

  2. 02

    As the evening falls

    The blue hour

    The shopfront

    The twenty minutes after sunset. The sky still has colour and the lit venue looks warm from the street. It is the photo that says you are open and that it is nice inside.

  3. 03

    Friday at ten, Sunday at lunchtime

    The busy hour

    Atmosphere, hands and people

    A full venue is when it makes sense. You don’t need to stay all night: twenty minutes are enough for the atmosphere, the hands and the people.

02 The light in the venue

What you already have switched on

A

Switch off the ceiling. Switch on the tables.

Ceiling light lights everything the same and leaves the venue flat, like an office. The atmosphere comes from the low lights: candles, table lamps, neon, the bottle display. Before you film, switch off whatever hangs from the ceiling and keep only what is at people’s height.

Darkness doesn’t hide your venue: it fills it.

Try it with the switches

Switches

Everything equally bright. It looks like an office.

B

For products, light from the side.

Put the product beside a window, not in front of it or behind it. The light comes in from one side: one side shines and the other falls into shadow. That is volume. The phone goes at ninety degrees to the window, facing the product.

Seen from above

What the phone sees

One side shines and the other falls into shadow. The product has volume.

C

On your phone: tap the light and lower the sun.

Tap the brightest part of the image on the screen (the candle, the glass, the window) and hold your finger until it locks. Then lower the brightness a touch with the sun or the slider that appears next to it. Everything else goes to black, and that’s fine: that is how the light that matters shows.

  3. 03Let the rest go to black

03 Framing

Three rules before you shoot

A

Get down to its height.

From above, everything looks like a set lunch menu. At the height of the glass, it looks like an advert. The simplest trick I know: rest the phone on the table and raise it slowly to the middle of the glass. From there you see the shape, the colour and what is behind.

What the phone sees

Advert

B

Remove whatever doesn’t pay rent.

Before you shoot, look at the edges of the frame: the napkin holder, the cables, the menu, the water bottle. If it isn’t the star, it goes. One object, a clean background and space around it.

0 / 4 out

C

Move in until half is left out.

A wide shot shows how many tables are empty. A detail shows what goes on inside. When you think you are close enough, take one more step: if you have left out half of what you were seeing, you are doing it right. With your feet, not the zoom.

  1. 01Wide shot: you count the empty tables
  2. 02Half left out: the people appear
  3. 03Detail: you see what is happening

04 The 10 shots

In the order I film them

The 10 shots I film in every venue.

Always in this order: from outside to inside and from small to big. Each shot has a place for the phone and a reason to be there. The wide shot goes last, when there are people.

Ten shots of ten seconds: less than two minutes of filming.

  1. 01

    The shopfront with life

    From the pavement opposite, with someone walking in. It says where you are and that there is life inside.

    Phone: At eye level

    10 s

  2. 02

    The door from inside

    The first thing people see when they come in: the street at the back and the lit venue around it. It is the welcome.

    Phone: At eye level

    10 s

  3. 03

    The bar from behind

    Behind the bar, at the height of the glasses, with whoever is serving in profile. It shows the craft from where nobody else can stand.

    Phone: At glass height

    10 s

  4. 04

    The hands

    Pouring, cutting, plating. No faces, just the gesture. It is the shot that sells most.

    Phone: At bar height

    10 s

  5. 05

    The star product, at its height

    The glass, the plate, the hookah bowl: the phone at the middle of the product and the light from the side. It is the photo people come looking for.

    Phone: At the middle of the product

    10 s

  6. 06

    The detail

    The foam, the crust, the ice, the grain of the wood. Move in until half is left out.

    Phone: Right up close

    10 s

  7. 07

    The shared gesture

    A toast, two hands passing something, the bill on the table. It says people come here together.

    Phone: At table height

    10 s

  8. 08

    Atmosphere in layers

    From the back of the venue, with someone out of focus in front and the table lights behind. Three layers give depth.

    Phone: Sitting down

    10 s

  9. 09

    The people who make it happen

    The team at work. If someone poses, give them something to hold: a tray, a cloth, a bottle.

    Phone: At eye level

    10 s

  10. 10

    The wide shot, last

    The whole venue, just once and with people. Never empty: that is why it goes last.

    Phone: From a corner, a little higher

    10 s

Film each shot for ten seconds without moving the phone. If you want movement, move yourself afterwards, in another take. With these ten you have material for a month of posts.

The shoot-day checklist

  • Clean the lens with your T-shirt.
  • Battery above 80 % and free space on the phone.
  • Aeroplane mode, so no call comes in halfway through a shot.
  • Switch off the ceiling lights and switch on lamps and candles.
  • Clear the tables of whatever doesn’t pay rent.
  • Let the team know you are going to film.

07 Questions

What people search for most

Short answers to the most common questions.

01What time should I photograph my bar?

It depends on what you want to show. Products and details in the morning, before opening and next to a window. The shopfront in the blue hour: the twenty minutes after sunset. The atmosphere at the busiest time, on a Friday night or at Sunday lunchtime. Twenty minutes at each moment is enough.

02Is my phone enough or do I need a camera?

For Instagram, a phone from the last few years is enough. What makes the difference is the time of day, the light and the height you film from, not the device. Clean the lens, lock the exposure and don’t use the zoom: move closer yourself.

03Can I film my customers?

Always ask first. If someone doesn’t want to appear, frame them so they can’t be recognised: hands, backs or people out of focus in the background. Recognisable faces only with permission. This isn’t legal advice: if you have doubts about a specific case, ask whoever handles data protection for your business.

04How many photos should I post from each shoot?

Far fewer than you take. Pick three per shot at most and spread the rest over time. With the ten shots in this guide you have material for a month without repeating yourself.

05Do I need extra lighting?

Hardly ever. Before buying anything, switch off the ceiling lights and use what you already have: the window in the morning, and the low lamps, candles and neon at night. If a plate or a glass looks too dark, move it closer to the window or a lamp before adding a light.

06How do I stop my venue looking empty on Instagram?

Film at the busiest time, move in close and leave the wide shot for last. Some hands or a toast show life even if only three tables are taken at that moment. And switch off the ceiling lights: with flat light, anywhere looks empty.

07How often should I film?

One shoot a month with the ten shots gives you enough to post every week. If you change the menu, the decor or the season, film again: it is the best excuse to show the venue once more.
