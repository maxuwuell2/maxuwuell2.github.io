06 Before you shoot

Three light rules

So that no crop ruins your photo.

  1. 01

    Shoot with room to spare.

    Leave space around the subject. That way the same photo fits vertical, square and horizontal formats without cutting what matters.

  2. 02

    Keep what matters in the centre.

    Every crop shares the centre. If your product is there, it survives any format (and any interface).

  3. 03

    Export at the exact size.

    Upload each photo at the size the network asks for, in sRGB. If you upload it bigger, the app compresses it its own way, and that is rarely yours.

08 Questions

What people search for most

Short answers to the most common questions.

01What size is an Instagram post in 2026?

The most used is the vertical 4:5, at 1080 × 1350 px. Since 2025 Instagram also accepts 3:4 (1080 × 1440) without cropping. Square is 1080 × 1080 and horizontal is 1080 × 566.

02What size are Stories and Reels?

1080 × 1920 px, vertical 9:16. The interface covers the top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% on each side: keep the text and the product in the centre.

03Why does Instagram crop my photos on my profile?

Since 2025 the profile grid shows vertical 3:4 thumbnails. A 4:5 photo loses about 34 px on each side; a square or horizontal one loses much more. If what matters is in the centre, you won't notice.

04What size is a YouTube thumbnail?

16:9 format. YouTube recommends 3840 × 2160 px and accepts anything from 640 px wide, so 1280 × 720 still works. Up to 50 MB if you upload it from a computer and 2 MB from a phone.

05What is a safe zone?

It is the part of the image that no interface covers. In vertical video, the buttons, the name and the text take up the edges; anything you put there may not be seen.

06What format should I export photos in for social media?

JPG in sRGB, at the exact size of the format (for example, 1080 px wide for Instagram). If you upload a much bigger photo, the app scales it down and compresses it its own way.
